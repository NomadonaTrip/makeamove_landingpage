# Make A Move: future-state copy (draft 2)

**The job of this page:** let a tired single person *see* their life after Make A Move clearly enough to want it today. The page never explains how the app works. It shows where the reader ends up.

**The promise, in one line:**
> Meet the one who wants you, and wants the life you want.

## Rules for every line on this page

1. **Show the after, not the how.** No questions, scores, rounds, games, phases, phones or features. If a sentence explains the product, cut it or turn it into the moment it creates.
2. **Write scenes, not claims.** Write "You sit across from someone who wants the same family you do," not "Find compatible partners."
3. **Second person, present tense.** The reader is already there: "You walk in," "You tell your friends."
4. **Concrete over abstract.** Name real things: Sunday lunch with family, a reply that comes the same evening, the phone you stop checking at midnight.
5. **Honest.** No invented numbers, testimonials or guarantees. Paint what's possible, and don't promise a wedding.

## 1 · Hero

**Headline:**
# Meet the one who wants you.

**Subheadline:**
> And wants the life you want. Picture a first date where the big things are already settled: the family, the faith, the home you'll build. All that's left is to enjoy each other.

**CTA:** Find your match

**Why it works:** it answers the swipe scene's two wounds, being ignored ("replies in a fortnight") and being deceived ("they're married"), with the feeling everyone on a dating app wants most: being chosen, for real. The subheadline then adds the second half of the promise, a shared future.

**Earlier options (not used):**
- *"Meet the one who wants the life you want."* This now lives in the subheadline.
- *"Stop swiping. Start matching."* (current page) It says what you *do*, not where you end up.

## 4 · Your life, after (replaces "How we find your match")

**Heading:** Your life, after

1. **Your evenings are yours again.**
   No more scrolling at midnight. No more waiting two weeks for a reply. When your phone lights up, it's them, and they mean it.

2. **You stop interviewing and start enjoying them.**
   The person across the table wants the same future you do: the family, the faith, the city you'll call home. The first date feels like the fifth.

3. **You say "I've met someone", and you mean it.**
   You tell your friends, and this time you're not hedging. You already know it's going somewhere.

**Closing line:**
> And they're exactly who they said they'd be.

## 5 · The first chapter (replaces "Game Ends. Date Begins.")

**Heading:** The guessing ends. **Your story begins.**

1. **The first date feels like a reunion.**

## Retired language

| Describes the product (cut) | Paints the future (use) |
|---|---|
| game show, rounds, players, the game | the first date, the drive home, Sunday with family |
| 20 questions, the hard questions, answering | you already know, you already agree |
| 70% threshold, match score, compatible | wants the same future, wants what you want |
| ID-verified, moderated | they're who they said they were, it feels respectful |
| private planning channels, date invite | you plan a future, not just the next date |
| play at your own pace, on your phone | your evenings are yours again |

**Note on the page itself:** the game-show visuals (the studio, the stamp, the LED sign) can stay as the brand's look. The words over them now describe the future. Where a visual already tells the story, such as the swipe scene, the copy stays short and lets the picture work.
